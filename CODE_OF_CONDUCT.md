## Our standard

Ward follows the **[Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/)**.
It applies in full, as if it were written out here. We link to it instead of copying it so that
you always read the current, authoritative text, and so that this file does not drift from it.
The Contributor Covenant is available under the
[Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/); it is
copyright the Contributor Covenant project, and we have not changed it.

In short, in our own words: everyone who takes part in Ward, in the repository, in issues and
pull requests, in any group the project runs and in public when speaking for it, is expected to
be kind, patient and respectful; to give and take feedback gracefully; to take responsibility
when they get something wrong; and to put what is best for the community, and the children it
exists for, ahead of being right. Harassment, insults, personal attacks, sexualised language or
attention, publishing other people's private information, and anything a reasonable person would
find inappropriate in a professional setting are not acceptable.

## Additions for a project that serves children

Ward is for children, and some people who take part in the project are young. These rules
come on top of the Covenant.

- **Never put a real child's information in the project.** Do not paste a real child's chat,
  name, photo, school or contact details into an issue, pull request or discussion, even with
  good intentions. Use made-up examples. Maintainers will hide or remove such content.
- **No content that sexualises or exploits a child.** Ever, including as a "test" or "example".
  If you see any, do not open it or share it; report it as described in
  [docs/SAFEGUARDING.md](docs/SAFEGUARDING.md#7-disclosures-and-concerns-that-reach-the-project).
- **Be careful with harmful language.** Slang packs and test data contain slurs and threats
  because the app has to recognise them. Keep them in the files where they belong, with their
  meaning noted, and do not use them against other contributors or as jokes.
- **Young contributors.** If you are under 18, you are welcome. Do not share your school,
  address or phone number in the project. If someone asks you for personal details, or for
  contact outside the project's public channels, say no and tell a maintainer.
- **Adults and young people.** Adults do not contact young contributors privately. Keep
  conversations in public issues or in group channels a second adult can see. Nobody should
  ask a young person for photos, personal stories or secrecy.
- **No pressure.** Nobody should be pushed to share their own experience of bullying or abuse in
  order to contribute.

## Scope

This code applies in all project spaces and when someone is representing the project in public.
It also applies to behaviour elsewhere that harms a project participant, particularly a young
one.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report a conduct concern privately to `[conduct contact address: to be created]`. If the concern
is about a maintainer, or you would rather not write to the maintainers, write to
`[second contact, outside the maintainer group: to be named]`. If it is about a child's safety,
see [docs/SAFEGUARDING.md](docs/SAFEGUARDING.md#10-raising-a-concern-about-the-project-itself).
If someone is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services first.

Reports are handled by the safeguarding lead (`[safeguarding lead: to be appointed]`) and one
other maintainer who is not involved. They will:

- acknowledge the report within `[number: for example 3 working days]`;
- treat it in confidence, sharing only what is needed to act;
- never ask the reporter to confront the person;
- decide on a response using the Covenant's enforcement ladder (a private note, a warning, a
  temporary ban, a permanent ban), and tell the reporter what was decided.

Content that breaks this code can be hidden, edited or removed, and the person responsible can
lose commit or participation rights. Until a safeguarding lead is appointed, the project steward
handles reports and asks another maintainer to review any decision.
