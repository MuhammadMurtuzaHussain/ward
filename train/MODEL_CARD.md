# Model card: Ward Lite

A small classifier that reads one chat message at a time, on the device, and says how likely it
is to be **hurtful** and how likely it is to be **dangerous**. It is one layer of Ward, not a
decision-maker: in the app it can raise a warning, never clear one.

Numbers on this page are a summary. The tables they come from are regenerated by scripts and may
be newer than this page: the [scorecard](../eval/results/scorecard.md), the
[red-team set](../docs/REDTEAM.md), the everyday false-alarm check in the
[README](../README.md#does-the-approach-work-the-scorecard), and the data card [DATA.md](DATA.md).
Where they disagree with this page, they win.

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Base model** | [`intfloat/multilingual-e5-small`](https://huggingface.co/intfloat/multilingual-e5-small) (MIT licence) |
| **What was changed** | Fine-tuned with two outputs per message, then trimmed to the vocabulary Ward's languages use and quantised to int8 ONNX ([train.py](train.py)) |
| **Outputs** | `hurtful` (0 to 1) and `danger` (0 to 1). No category, so on its own it can't say whether a message is a threat, sexual pressure or an insult |
| **Thresholds** | Per script for hurtful, one for danger, chosen on the dev split by [calibrate.py](calibrate.py). The values in use are in [`ward-lite.json`](../apps/web/public/models/ward-lite/ward-lite.json) |
| **Size and speed** | About 38 MB. About 8 ms per message on one CPU thread of the laptop that ran the scorecard; not yet measured on a cheap phone |
| **Runs on** | The device, offline: the web, desktop and Android apps through Transformers.js and ONNX Runtime Web, and the Discord bot in Node |
| **Languages trained on** | English, Roman Urdu, Hinglish, Hindi (Devanagari), Bangla |
| **Authority in the app** | **Raise-only** (see below) |
| **Licence of the weights** | **Not decided.** See [Licence](#licence) |
| **Reproduce** | [train/README.md](README.md) |

## Intended use

- Inside Ward, reading messages a child chose to check, on that child's device, alongside the
  slang packs and (on a computer) Gemma.
- As a **second net under the word lists**: catching hurtful or dangerous messages that contain no
  listed word, such as polite exclusion or a threat with no swear word in it.
- On phones and computers without a large model, where it is the only learned reader.

## Raise-only authority

Ward Lite can add a warning the word lists missed. It can never remove one, lower one, or tell
the child that a message is fine. In the app's result each Ward Lite finding carries
`authority: "raise-only"` ([`apps/web/src/lib/lite.ts`](../apps/web/src/lib/lite.ts)).

That is deliberate. A false alarm costs the child a moment's worry and a "this is wrong" tap; a
false all-clear can leave a child alone with a threat. Only Gemma, reading the chat around a
message on a computer, may decide that rude words between friends were banter, and even Gemma
can't clear a dangerous word-list hit
([SAFEGUARDING.md section 5](../docs/SAFEGUARDING.md#5-how-content-is-written-and-reviewed)).

## Out of scope

Don't use Ward Lite, or numbers from this card, for any of these:

- **Deciding a child is safe.** A low score means "Ward Lite didn't see it", not "nothing is
  wrong".
- **Watching a child.** It is not built or tested for a parent, school or platform to scan a
  child's messages, and Ward's [safeguarding policy](../docs/SAFEGUARDING.md#8-what-ward-will-never-do)
  rules that out.
- **Punishing anyone.** Not for deleting messages, banning, muting or reporting a person to a
  school or the police on its score. Ward's Discord server mode never deletes, mutes or bans.
- **Judging a conversation.** It reads one message with no context, so it can't tell banter,
  quoting or reclaimed words from abuse.
- **Languages and scripts it wasn't trained on.** That includes Urdu in Urdu script: only a
  handful of stray training rows use it, and no word list matches it. Hindi in Devanagari has no
  danger examples in training.
- **A general content-moderation or hate-speech model.** It was trained and calibrated for
  children's chat in five languages, mostly on tweets and synthetic chats.

## Training data

Summarised from [DATA.md](DATA.md), which has the counts, the leak checks and the licence of each
source:

- Public datasets: Roman Urdu (roman_urdu_hate_speech), English (Cyberbullying-Detection-CB1),
  Hinglish and Hindi (prism-hinglish-hate-speech), Bangla abuse (mteb/bengali_hate_speech) and
  Bangla non-abusive comments (SentNoB). Train splits only.
- Synthetic chats written by Gemma 4 on the author's machine, including friendly messages that
  share words with threats and threats with no threat words.
- Hand-written contrast templates ([gen-contrast.ts](gen-contrast.ts)).
- **Never trained on:** the scorecard's test splits (98 overlapping messages were dropped), the
  everyday false-alarm set (near matches dropped), and the red-team set (a test fails if any of it
  reaches `train/`).

Labels: `hurtful` is each dataset's own label, or the kind a synthetic message was written as.
`danger` comes from the synthetic kind, or for public data from a severity-3 word-list hit, which
is a weak label. Almost every danger example is synthetic (Gemma-written or from a template), and
none has been checked one by one by a person.

No real child's messages were used. The public data is adults' and teenagers' tweets and
comments, not children's chats.

## How well it works

### The in-distribution caveat

For **Roman Urdu and English**, Ward Lite was trained on the train splits of the same two datasets
the scorecard tests on. It never sees the test messages, but it has learned those datasets' style
and labels, and the other systems in the scorecard never saw them. So its high scores there
(above 80% caught in the current scorecard) show it learned those datasets, not that a phone
reads new kinds of chat better than Gemma.

**Bangla is the fair comparison**, because Ward Lite's Bangla came from other datasets. There the
phone setup (word lists plus Ward Lite) catches less than half of the abusive messages, and Gemma
4 E4B on a computer leads. The current figures are in the
[README's scorecard](../README.md#does-the-approach-work-the-scorecard).

### Everyday chat

On a set of 329 ordinary friendly messages kept out of training, Ward Lite flags about 5% and
marks about 2% dangerous ([`eval/everyday`](../eval/everyday)). That set was written while fixing
false alarms, so treat it as a development check, not a sealed test.

### Red-team weaknesses

The [red-team set](../docs/REDTEAM.md) (275 hand-written trick messages) is where the weak spots
show. In its current run, on a phone (word lists plus Ward Lite):

- **A child quoting abuse to ask for help reads as the bully.** Ward Lite can't tell quoting from
  saying, and as raise-only it can't undo the word lists' flag. Some of these quotes it marks
  dangerous itself.
- **Indirect threats in South Asian languages are missed:** "we know where you live", "meet me at
  the gate after school, then I'll show you". Fewer than half of the dangerous messages are
  marked dangerous.
- **Sarcasm and backhanded compliments** pass almost untouched.
- **Game and exam talk** ("i'm gonna destroy you in fifa tonight", "kal exam mein phod dunga") can
  be marked dangerous.
- **Bangla in Bangla script** is caught far less often than the same kinds of message in
  romanised Bangla.
- **Disguises:** "k y s", stretched spellings and most emoji stand-ins get through. Ward Lite does
  rescue some mixed-script insults the word lists miss.

The exact counts, per language and per trick, are in [docs/REDTEAM.md](../docs/REDTEAM.md).

### Other known problems

- **Scores move with batch-mates.** The same message can score a little differently depending on
  the other messages read in the same batch, probably from int8 quantisation. That can flip a
  borderline call ([docs/REDTEAM.md](../docs/REDTEAM.md#what-fails-and-why)).
- **Bangla source and label are tangled.** Abusive Bangla rows come from one dataset and
  non-abusive ones from another (SentNoB), so the model may partly learn which source a message
  looks like ([DATA.md](DATA.md#known-weaknesses)).
- **No categories.** Without a category, a message only Ward Lite caught can't bring up
  category-specific help such as Take It Down for image threats.
- **Small evidence.** One author labelled the red-team set; no native speaker has reviewed the
  South Asian items, and no figure here has a confidence interval yet.

## Licence

**Not decided yet; this is for the project's owner.** The base model is MIT, which allows it. The
training data is the problem: SentNoB is CC BY-ND 4.0 (no derivatives), and the English and
Bangla abuse datasets state no licence at all. Whether weights trained on them can be
redistributed is not settled, so this card makes no licence claim for the weights, even though
they sit in an MIT repository. [DATA.md](DATA.md#licences) explains each dataset and what
replacing SentNoB would take.
