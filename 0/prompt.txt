  ### 1. Cyberbullying is still common and may be rising, while public talk has moved to "brain rot"
  **Verdict: Supported.** The rise is clear in the WHO/Europe data and in Australian complaints. The US numbers are higher still but come with caveats.
  - 15% of 11-, 13- and 15-year-olds (about 1 in 6) were cyberbullied. Boys went from 12% in 2018 to 15%, and girls from 13% to 16%. School bullying "remained stable". The sample was over 279,000 young people in 44 countries. Source: "One in six school-aged children experiences cyberbullying, finds new WHO/Europe study", WHO/Europe, 27 Mar 2024. https://www.who.int/europe/news/item/27-03-2024-one-in-six-school-aged-children-experiences-cyberbullying--finds-new-who-europe-study
    - Caveat: secondary coverage dates the fieldwork to 2021/22.
  - US teens aged 13–17 in May 2025: about 58% cyberbullied at some point and about 33% in the last 30 days. 16.1% said they had cyberbullied someone in the last 30 days. Boys were targeted more than girls (36.6% vs 28.6%). Source: "2025 Cyberbullying Data", Cyberbullying Research Center (Patchin), 30 Jun 2025. https://cyberbullying.org/2025-cyberbullying-data
    - Caveat: US only, n=3,466, 20% response rate.
  - Australia: valid cyberbullying reports to eSafety rose from 536 in 2019 to 2,978 in 2024, an increase of 455%. 46% of the 2024 reports (1,385) concerned children aged 13 or under. Source: eSafety media release "Safer Internet Day calls for kindness as cyberbullying reports surge over 450% in five years", 11 Feb 2025, read via the Medianet copy. https://newshub.medianet.com.au/2025/02/safer-internet-day-calls-for-kindness-as-cyberbullying-reports-surge-over-450-in-five-years/86890
  - The shift in public talk: Oxford named "brain rot" its 2024 Word of the Year. Use of the term rose 230% and it drew more than 37,000 votes. Source: The Daily Star, 2 Dec 2024. https://www.thedailystar.net/tech-startup/news/brain-rot-oxfords-2024-word-the-year-what-does-it-even-mean-3766761
  - AI is making it worse:
    - 1 in 8 young people aged 13–20 know someone targeted by deepfake nudes, and 1 in 5 teens think making them is legal. Source: Thorn, 3 Mar 2025. https://www.thorn.org/press-releases/1-in-8-teens-know-someone-targeted-by-deepfake-nudes-new-report-finds/
    - NCMEC received more than 50,000 financial sextortion reports in 2025, about 137 a day, up from more than 36,000 in 2024. Source: NCMEC, 2026. https://banner.missingkids.org/blog/2026/ncmec-releases-new-sextortion-data-2025

  ### 2. Few governments act, and what exists is mostly bans and controls that don't teach children
  **Verdict: Partly supported.** Bans exist and leak. "Few governments" is too strong, though; see the flag below.

Source: "Australia's under-16 social media ban failing, study shows", Al Jazeera, 3 Aug 2026. https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/3/australias-under-16-social-media-ban-failing-study-shows-what-it-means

  ### 3. Children don't know what counts as bullying, and don't tell parents
  **Verdict: Supported**, but most of the evidence is from 2022.
  - 60% of Irish 8–12-year-olds would never tell their parents about cyberbullying, and almost half would tell no one. The reasons they gave: fear that parents would take the phone away, and fear of losing friends. Source: "60% of children would not tell parents about cyberbullying", RTÉ (reporting a Barnardos survey), 29 Sep 2022. https://www.rte.ie/news/ireland/2022/0929/1326059-cyber-bullying-survey/
  - 62% of non-victims said they would tell a parent about deepfake nudes, but only 34% of actual victims did. Source: Thorn, 2025 (URL above).
  - Children and adolescents "have a limited ability to apply a given standard" definition of bullying and cyberbullying to real-life scenarios. This ability varied by age. Source: Campbell & Xu, "Children and adolescents' understanding of traditional and cyberbullying", *Children and Youth Services Review* vol. 139, 2022 (abstract only). https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/cysrev/v139y2022ics0190740922001645.html
  
  ### 4. South Asian children text in Roman Urdu, Hinglish and Bangla, and safety tools are mostly English
  **Verdict: Partly supported.** There is good evidence that moderation tools are weak in these languages. I found no source with figures on how many children text in Roman script.

Large language models "frequently misclassify code-mixed content". Source: Shahid, Elswah & Vashistha, "Think Outside the Data…", arXiv 2501.13836, Jan 2025. https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2501.13836

  - Pakistan: the Digital Rights Foundation helpline logged 3,012 new complaints in 2025, of which 2,586 were cyber harassment. Cases involving minors rose 28%, from 124 to 159. WhatsApp accounted for 34% of cases. Source: The Friday Times, 15 Apr 2026. https://www.thefridaytimes.com/15-Apr-2026/rise-cases-involving-minors-flags-urgent-child-online-safety-crisis-digital-rights-foundation-helpline-report-2025
  - Bangladesh: 32% of children aged 10–17 face cyberbullying (n=1,281, UNICEF), and 63% go online mainly from their own room. Source: The Daily Star, 5 Feb 2019. https://www.thedailystar.net/country/safer-internet-day-2019-prevent-bullying-of-children-online-in-bangladesh-unicef-1697785
